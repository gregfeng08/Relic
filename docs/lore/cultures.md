# Cultures

Seven peoples of the Wide, one at home in each World region family. Near borders and passes, a region can blend two cultures. The structured versions live in `Assets/Relic/Lore/Data/Cultures/*.json`. Those files are the source of truth; this page is the readable tour.

| Culture | Home family (also found in) | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| **Wendle** | Meadow (Forest) | Farmers, millers and bakers who think most problems improve with a pie. |
| **Aruvi** | Forest (Marsh) | Woodwrights and grove-tenders who share their villages with spirits. |
| **Teskari** | Highlands (Frost) | Terrace-builders, tea growers, bellfounders and kite-flyers. |
| **Solanne** | Coast (Meadow) | Sunny harbor folk who keep the Wide's lighthouses lit. |
| **Ochrel** | Badlands (Highlands) | Mesa potters, glassblowers and star-readers who run the great caravans. |
| **Eskeln** | Frost (Coast) | Northern herders and storytellers who measure winters in tales. |
| **Lunnet** | Marsh (Coast) | Stilt-village ferry-keepers famous for frog choirs and terrible puns. |

**How names work.** Each culture has three syllable sets (starts, middles, ends) and weighted patterns such as `SE` (start + end) or `SME`. Place names add a bound suffix to a short root (*Bram* + *fold*) or a free word after a full root (*Selaru Grove*). Every example name below comes straight from the generator (people from seeds 1–6, places from seeds 1–4).

## The Wendle (Meadow)
*The heart of the Rekindling. The Lanternfold is a Wendle town.*

- **Names.** Short, cozy English-sounding syllables. Starts: *Bram, Tam, Wil, Pell, Fen, Tib, Maud, Hal…*; ends: *-wick, -sin, -ley, -by, -kin, -ett, -row…*; often a family name. Places take *-fold, -wick, -ford, -mere, -stead, -dale* or a word like *Green, Mill, Downs, Crossing*.
  *Examples:* Fenrow, Wilbet Osrow, Hal Halton, Corokin, Alrow, Wila Gilsin, Fenbetstead, Fenfold, Betawen Downs, Nel Crossing.
- **Lands.** Rolling meadow and the edges of the forests; mild, temperate weather.
- **Architecture.** Half-timbered cottages under deep thatch, round doors painted bright colors, a windmill on every rise, and stone bridges with a bench in the middle for talking.
- **Crafts and materials.** Baking, milling, cheesemaking, beekeeping, lantern-making, quilting, cider. Oak, straw, beeswax, brass, wool, river stone.
- **Food and festivals.** Meat-and-onion pies, honey cake, hard cheese, cider. *The Lantern-Lighting* (one lantern per family member who traveled that year), *the Haymoot* (hay-bale races and a pie judging that has ended friendships), *First Furrow*.
- **Values and pride.** Hospitality, neighborliness, honest work, a good story, being home before dark. Proud of the Old Hearth, and that no traveler has ever left a Wendle inn hungry.
- **Factions.** Farming households, cider houses, fair committees, Reliquary keepers, Hearthwardens, wayfarer companies.
- **Items.** Lanterns, walking staffs, shepherd's crooks, sickles, cleavers, wool cloaks, quilted coats, longbows, slings, and the infamous *pie-tin buckler*. Motifs: lantern, bloom, hive, bell, song, thorn.
- **Item names.** "[Maker]'s [Form]", "The [Adjective] [Form]" (*Good, Steady, Mended, Well-Loved*), "[Form] of [Place]", "The [Epithet] [Form]" (*Haymoot, Hearthside, Long-Rail*). Maker's marks: a stamped wheat-ear, a tiny pie burned into the wood.
- **Relations.** Pie rivals with the Lunnet; trade grain for Aruvi honey (and receive free lectures about trees); wool to the Solanne for salt and glass; brew Teskari tea far too strong.

## The Aruvi (Forest)
*Nobody tends the wild like the Aruvi. A grove they look after never goes wild.*

- **Names.** Flowing and vowel-rich, usually three syllables, no family names (people are known by their grove). Starts: *A, I, Se, Li, Me, Ru, Va, Ela…*; middles: *ru, la, vi, no, sa…*; ends: *-vi, -ai, -ue, -oru, -ani, -eli…*. Places: *-ashe, -lun, -avel, -ori, -esse*, or *Grove, Canopy, Glade, Boughs, Rootway*.
  *Examples:* Oriaru, Vauvi, Lilauvi, Nelaru, Lomeon, Seyaruan, Haoru Boughs, Isail Glade, Lianlun, Suruluvi Boughs.
- **Lands.** Humid forested hills, spilling into the marsh edges.
- **Architecture.** Houses grown rather than built: living-willow walls, rope bridges between great trees, lacquered shrines among the roots, moth-lanterns on every branch.
- **Crafts and materials.** Woodcarving, lacquerwork, bow-making, herbal medicine, moth-silk weaving, mushroom farming. Living willow, red lacquer, moth-silk, amberwood, birch bark, river pearl.
- **Food and festivals.** Mushroom dumplings, nut bread, flower tea, smoked river fish. *Moth Lantern Night* (wish-carved lanterns float up into the canopy), *the Tending* (asking the grove's patron spirit, very politely, how its year went), *First Leaf*.
- **Values and pride.** Patience, care for living things, listening, taking only what you need. Proud that their spirits trust them.
- **Factions.** Shrine keepers, glyph circles, growers, Reliquary keepers.
- **Items.** Carved longbows, lacquered staffs, moth-silk cloaks, seed-pouch charms, bark-bound tomes, antler knives, spirit masks, wooden flutes. Motifs: thorn, bloom, lantern, mist, ink, hive.
- **Item names.** "[Form] of the [Word]" (*Bow of the Bramble*), "[Maker]'s [Form]", "The [Adjective] [Form]" (*Patient, Living, Evergreen*). Marks: a leaf carved so finely it seems to move; a seed-shaped drop of red lacquer.
- **Relations.** Kin to the Lunnet (their lantern festivals share an evening); trade partners of the Wendle; a polite, unending tea debate with the Teskari.

## The Teskari (Highlands)
*The bridges they built between peaks in the Lamplit Age are still standing. They will mention this.*

- **Names.** Crisp two- and three-syllable names, usually with a one-syllable family name. Starts: *Tes, Kar, Yen, Sho, Hal, Mir, Ket, Sen, Ore…*; ends: *-i, -an, -ek, -ari, -esh, -el…*. Places: *-kel, -orin, -shan, -dar, -yen*, or *Steps, Terrace, Spire, Bridge, Pass*.
  *Examples:* Teso, Kartean Tes, Tamo, Doryao, Halesh, Oreshiari Kar, Yenel Pass, Yushan, Halenshan, Yuel Spire.
- **Lands.** Tall ridged mountains and cliffs, up to the snowline.
- **Architecture.** Stone terraces stepping down every slope, tiled roofs that curl up at the corners like a smile, bell towers on the crags, rope-and-plank bridges strung between peaks.
- **Crafts and materials.** Bellfounding, tea growing, stone carving, kite making, porcelain, paper. Granite, bell-bronze, porcelain, kite-silk, mountain cedar, slate.
- **Food and festivals.** Terrace tea, steamed buns, goat cheese, pine-nut noodles, plum wine. *The Kite Ascent* (the highest kite wins a year of bragging), *Bellnight* (every bell in the range rings once at midnight, peak after peak), *Tea Moon*.
- **Values and pride.** Patience, precision, keeping one's word, climbing a little higher each year. Proud of their bells, their tea, and Oren Kel the Bellfounder.
- **Factions.** Bellfounders, smiths, glyph circles, lamplighters, mountain caravans.
- **Items.** Bells, kite-blades (light curved swords), cedar staffs, porcelain masks, climbing picks, war-fans, lacquered lamellar, scroll cases. Motifs: bell, wind, stone, ink, frost.
- **Item names.** "[Form] of the [Word]", "[Maker]'s [Form]", "The [Epithet] [Form]" (*Peak-Rung, Ninth-Step, Cloud-Stair*). Marks: a tiny bell engraved where the hand rests; the number of steps from the maker's door.
- **Relations.** The yearly Peak Race against the Eskeln; tea and slate traded down to the Ochrel for glass; their best tea customers are the Wendle.

## The Solanne (Coast)
*Their lighthouses kept burning through the whole of the Hush.*

- **Names.** Sunny, musical names with a family name, often ending in a vowel. Starts: *Cor, Ama, Lis, Vel, Ser, Fio, Ren…*; ends: *-o, -a, -ello, -ine, -etta, -ano, -iel…*. Places: *-port, -mar, -ello, -ara, -esca*, or *Harbor, Light, Strand, Cove, Quay, Point*.
  *Examples:* Roane Velina, Fioliello Velis, Nelane, Renneine Luiel, Ottaina, Amaraina Corello, Belezo Point, Luveon Light, Tesinello, Renliel Strand.
- **Lands.** Low coasts falling into the sea; the meadow behind the dunes.
- **Architecture.** Whitewashed houses with blue shutters stacked up the cliffs, lighthouses crowned with great glass lenses, markets spilling along the quays.
- **Crafts and materials.** Shipbuilding, lens-grinding, nets and sails, glassblowing, salt-panning. Sea-glass, driftwood, sailcloth, salt, brass, coral, white stone.
- **Food and festivals.** Grilled sardines, saffron fish stew, lemon bread, sea-salt caramels. *The Regatta of Lights* (the harbor cheers for whoever is losing), *Lens Day*, *the Homecoming Tide* (bread, music and dry socks for the returning fleet).
- **Values and pride.** Courage, generosity, a well-told story, never turning a stranger away from the quay. Proud of their lighthouses and of Captain Velisa Corro.
- **Factions.** Shipwrights, cartographers, sea traders, fair committees, wayfarer companies.
- **Items.** Cutlasses, harpoons, spyglasses, compasses, lighthouse lanterns, sailcloth coats, sea-glass charms, oar-staffs. Motifs: tide, sun, star, wind, mist.
- **Item names.** "[Maker]'s [Form]", "[Form] of [Place]", "The [Adjective] [Form]" (*Bold, Salt-Kissed, Steadfast*), "The [Epithet] [Form]" (*Regatta, Lighthouse, Fair-Weather*). Marks: a tiny etched lighthouse; the maker's ship-name stamped backward, for luck.
- **Relations.** Trade partners and lens-invention rivals of the Ochrel; the Great Wager against the Eskeln; salt and glass inland to the Wendle.

## The Ochrel (Badlands)
*Their caravans kept the Long Roads alive when nobody else would walk them.*

- **Names.** Warm, open syllables; a family name about half the time. Starts: *Az, Ka, Mir, Ru, Sa, Tel, Zan, Nesh, Qa…*; ends: *-im, -esh, -ara, -un, -ad, -ani, -oum, -ah…*. Places: *-kar, -ad, -im, -esh*, or *Mesa, Kiln, Wells, Bluffs, Spire, Oasis*.
  *Examples:* Ruzeul, Zanesh, Azmaim, Haez Tela, Kair, Telraoum Baim, Baim, Bakad Spire, Azoum Spire, Telesh.
- **Lands.** Hot, dry, terraced mesas of red clay, climbing into the highland foothills.
- **Architecture.** Cool adobe towns carved into mesa walls, wind-towers that pull the breeze indoors, kilns glowing at dusk, shady courtyards around deep wells.
- **Crafts and materials.** Pottery, glassblowing, star-charting, caravan-keeping, dyeing, sun-clocks. Red clay, sun-glass, ochre dye, copper, sandstone, dune-goat wool.
- **Food and festivals.** Flatbread baked on hot stones, spiced lentils, date cakes, mint tea. *The Night of Open Kilns* (every kiln opened at once, pots traded by firelight), *the Long Shade* (sleep through the hottest day, party all night), *the Star Reckoning* (a new calendar, announced very slightly wrong on purpose so there is something to argue about).
- **Values and pride.** Hospitality to travelers, careful water-keeping, reading the sky, bargaining well. Proud of their caravans and of glass so clear you forget it's there.
- **Factions.** Caravan companies, glyph circles, smiths, Reliquary keepers, and the occasional greedy toll-house.
- **Items.** Curved glaives, slings, glass orbs, star-chart tomes, sun-mirror shields, clay flasks, desert veils, copper bracers. Motifs: sun, ember, star, stone.
- **Item names.** "[Form] of [Place]", "[Maker]'s [Form]", "The [Adjective] [Form]" (*Clear, Sun-Warmed, Bright-Glazed*), "[Form] of the [Word]". Marks: a thumbprint pressed into the clay before firing; a small star etched into the glass.
- **Relations.** Lens rivals and spice partners of the Solanne; glass for Teskari tea; star-chart pen pals of the Eskeln; rush paper from the Lunnet by a very long road.

## The Eskeln (Frost)
*A winter is measured by how many stories it took to get through.*

- **Names.** Sturdy Northern names, often with a family name ending in a place (*Osvik*, *Ulfholm*). Starts: *Hja, Os, Bryn, Tor, Sve, Yr, Ask, Ing, Ulf…*; ends: *-a, -e, -ild, -ven, -rik, -dis, -holm, -vik…*. Places: *-holm, -vik, -stad, -fell, -hof*, or *Lodge, Hall, Firth, Ridge, Hearth, Floe*.
  *Examples:* Ingdavi, Askun Osvik, Ingke, Ingvedis Haby, Yrra Via, Brynvi Hae, Lifell, Yrkehof, Kaildstad, Askholm Hearth.
- **Lands.** Cold mountains, tundra and snow, down to the grey northern coasts.
- **Architecture.** Long turf-roofed halls half sunk into the hillside, steaming bathhouses, doorposts carved with family stories, snow-lanterns along every path.
- **Crafts and materials.** Bone and antler carving, wool-felting, sledge-building, ice-fishing, saga-telling, fur-lined leather. Whalebone, antler, felted wool, birch, rime-iron, amber.
- **Food and festivals.** Smoked fish, cloudberry jam, rye flatbread, reindeer stew, hot spiced milk. *The Long Telling* (the whole hall tells one story, a line each, for as long as the night lasts), *Sun-Return* (cheering the first sunrise, loudly, in case it needs encouragement), *the Thaw Market*.
- **Values and pride.** Endurance, hospitality in hard weather, a story told well, sharing the warm spot by the fire. Proud of their stories and of Hjalla Osvik, who is *definitely* winning the Great Wager.
- **Factions.** Wayfarer companies, Hearthwardens, fair committees, smiths, shrine keepers.
- **Items.** Bearded axes, antler-hilted knives, sledge-spears, whalebone bows, fur cloaks, horns, saga-staffs, round shields. Motifs: frost, song, star, tide.
- **Item names.** "[Maker]'s [Form]", "[Form] of [Place]", "[Form] of the [Word]", "The [Epithet] [Form]" (*Long-Telling, Fireside, Northward*). Marks: a tally of winters notched along the edge; a twist of red wool through the pommel.
- **Relations.** The Peak Race against the Teskari; the Great Wager against the Solanne; star charts swapped with the Ochrel.

## The Lunnet (Marsh)
*Nobody has ever successfully invaded a marsh. Nobody has ever out-punned a Lunnet either.*

- **Names.** Round, bouncy, slightly silly names, half with family names. Starts: *Pib, Lul, Nim, Tob, Wob, Mog, Pud, Bub, Sib, Mim…*; ends: *-et, -ow, -y, -kin, -ling, -le, -ick, -sy…*. Places: *-pond, -reed, -wade, -mire, -fen*, or *Stilts, Reeds, Ferry, Lilypads, Locks, Landing*.
  *Examples:* Bokin Lulo, Mimkin, Fenow, Duno Lille, Rushwiow Woble, Wimlisy Boap, Pibowade, Pibwade, Rushboick Locks, Sibkin Stilts.
- **Lands.** Flat, wet country of ponds and channels, out to the tidal flats.
- **Architecture.** Round reed-thatched houses on stilts, joined by plank walkways and little ferries, with a floating garden tied up outside every door.
- **Crafts and materials.** Reed-weaving, ferry-building, herbal medicine, eel-trapping, rush paper, gourd lanterns. Reeds, bog-oak, cattail down, gourd, river clay, willow.
- **Food and festivals.** Eel pie, duck-egg custard, rice cakes, watercress soup, cranberry tarts. *The Frog Choir* (the marsh sings back to the frogs; the frogs usually win), *the Firefly Ferry* (drifting boats, gourd-lanterns, everyone's worst joke), *the Big Dry* (hunting the mud for everything lost all year).
- **Values and pride.** Good humor, helping first and asking later, never laughing at a joke too early, knowing every channel. Proud of ferries that find any channel in thick fog.
- **Factions.** Growers, cooks, fair committees, shrine keepers, ferry-builders.
- **Items.** Punt-pole staffs, fishing spears, reed shields, gourd lanterns, herb satchels, tridents, eel-skin boots, rush-paper scrolls. Motifs: mist, tide, bloom, song, lantern.
- **Item names.** "[Maker]'s [Form]", "The [Adjective] [Form]" (*Cheerful, Lucky, Trusty, Muddy*), "[Form] of [Place]", "The [Epithet] [Form]" (*Frog-Choir, Firefly, Fog-Finder*). Marks: a little frog carved somewhere hard to find; three dots of green paint.
- **Relations.** Eel pie against Wendle meat pie, judged every Haymoot, never settled; kin to the Aruvi; rush paper for Ochrel glass.

## Blending at borders
Where two regions meet, the world graph may give a place both cultures. Rules of thumb:
- **Names** come from the culture that founded the place; the other culture's word may follow (*Fenfold Glade*).
- **Architecture** mixes kits (a Wendle mill with Aruvi lacquered shutters).
- **Festivals** merge, and usually get louder.
- **Relations** set the flavor: Kinship and Trade borders are friendly market towns; FriendlyRivalry borders host the races, contests and pie judgings.
