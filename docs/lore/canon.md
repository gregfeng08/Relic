# Canon

The fixed truths of Relic's world. Everything generated (regions, factions, relics, bosses, NPC talk) must agree with this page. Each truth comes with the explanation a character inside the world would give (pillar 7: *could a character explain why this works?*).

The world has no single official name. Most people call the known lands and everything past them **the Wide**: "out in the Wide," "the Wide is bigger than your map."

---

## 1. The Listening

**The truth:** the world pays attention. Scholars call this attention **the Listening**. Farmers say "the world's got its ear on it."

Everything that exists is heard a little. Things that are heard a lot (used every day, named, sung about, cared for, argued over) are heard more clearly. Over long years that attention settles into the thing itself as **resonance**: a faint warmth, a hum you notice only when the room is quiet.

Almost all magic in the Wide comes from this. Magic is not a private gift. It's the world's own attention, and the world listens to everyone.

**What it is not:** the Listening is not a god and does not judge. It doesn't love good people or punish bad ones. It notices, and it remembers what it noticed.

---

## 2. Relics: why old, loved objects hold power

**The truth:** a **relic** is an object the world has listened to for so long that it has begun to answer.

A ferry bell rung every dawn for three hundred years. A grandmother's bread knife that fed five generations. A map annotated by a hundred hands. None of these were made to be magical. They became magical by being *used and loved* until the Listening knew them by heart.

**Why a relic does what it does:** resonance takes the shape of what was heard. A bell that called fishing boats home learns to *pull things close*. A kiln-spade that held back fire for a century *shrugs off burning*. A relic's history is literally its enchantment, so its lore always explains its powers.

**Why relics lead somewhere (pillar 2):** a relic hums a little louder when it's carried toward the places it remembers. Every wayfarer learns to hold an old trinket up to the wind and feel which way it leans. That is why a relic's lore names real places, and why following it works.

**Rarity:** most objects never get there. It takes generations of use, or one extraordinary story told by many people. So relics are rare, and they're usually old, but they don't have to be grim. Most relics were loved, not mourned.

## 3. Essences and the Soulbinder

**The truth:** a creature or keeper that did *one thing* for a very long time gathers a deep resonance of that habit. When it is defeated, the habit doesn't vanish. It condenses into an **essence**: a small, warm bead, often like amber, frosted glass or river-stone, that holds the habit itself.

- The great owl that guarded a library for four centuries leaves the **patience of a reader** (INT).
- The stone giant who held up a fallen bridge leaves **strength** (STR).
- The blind ferryman spirit who knew every current leaves **insight** (INS).

This is why players can "read a boss" for what it will give: the essence is whatever the creature *was*.

**Why essences bind to souls:** a **soul** is the Listening's own note of who you are, the page the world keeps about you. An unbound essence is a habit with no owner, and it wants one. A **Soulbinder** (a trained sage at every sanctuary) speaks a short, careful glyph sentence over you and the essence: *let this be theirs.* The world turns the page, writes the habit in, and from then on hears you as a little stronger, wiser or quicker. So you are.

**No visible marks:** nothing appears on the body, because nothing changed on the body. The change is in how the world hears you. Soulbinders like to say "a soul has no skin." Instead, the Soulbinder keeps a record (the **Soul** screen) of every essence you've taken in and where it came from.

**Limits:** a page has margins. Each habit can only be written in so deeply, which is why each stat has a cap.

**Not every boss is a villain.** Many are guardians grown huge in forgotten places, or old constructs still at a post nobody remembers assigning. Defeating them frees the place and lets the Listening hear it clearly again.

## 4. Glyph magic and the Magister

**The truth:** anyone can learn magic, because the magic isn't in the caster. It's in the world, and the world *reads*.

A **glyph** is a word in the world's oldest grammar: *water*, *stone*, *outward*, *then*, *when struck*. A circle of glyphs drawn in good ink is a **sentence**. When a sentence is clear, the world does what it says, drawing on the caster's **mana** (their own focused attention, which grows with study).

That is why:
- **Anyone can cast.** You don't need a bloodline or a talent, just the vocabulary and a steady hand.
- **Clean drawing matters.** A sloppy circle is a mumbled sentence. The world still tries, but it costs more and sometimes misfires.
- **Glyphs are discovered.** The grammar is old and largely forgotten. New words are found carved in ruins, written in old spell pages, or taught by a patient teacher. Some of the oldest glyphs only make sense to someone with a lot of Insight.

**The Magister** is the name scholars gave to the part of the Listening that reads sentences. Think of it as the world's reader: courteous, literal, endlessly patient, and completely impartial. It has never been seen, only answered.

Three things everyone knows about the Magister:
1. **It never reads the same sentence two ways.** Once it decides what a drawing means, that meaning is fixed forever, for everyone. (In the game, this is the interpretation cache.)
2. **It reads anything well-formed**, however long or strange. It just takes the cost out of you, so a grand sentence is an expensive one.
3. **It does not take orders.** Sentences that try to *command* the Listening, rather than ask it, are the one kind it refuses. The last people who forced the question started a war (see the Quarrel).

A favorite scribe's joke: *the Magister has read every circle ever drawn and has never once complained about anyone's handwriting. It just charges for it.*

## 5. Graves, permadeath and the Reliquary

**The truth:** the Wide is generous, but it isn't gentle. People who go far sometimes don't come back.

**Why your belongings stay where you fall:** because things are only things. Nothing in the Wide carries a fallen wayfarer's pack home. No spirit fetches it and no spell recalls it. Your sword, your tome and your hand-drawn journal map stay exactly where you dropped them.

By custom, whoever finds a fallen traveler stacks a few stones and ties a ribbon to the top: a **waycairn**. Waycairns are never robbed out of spite, but the Wide's oldest courtesy says the next wayfarer may carry the contents onward, "so the road isn't wasted."

**Why there is a next wayfarer:** a wayfarer is not one undying hero. Each is a new person who sets out from the Lanternfold, carrying a lantern lit from the one before.

**The Reliquary** is how knowledge survives. Every sanctuary keeps one: a library-shrine run by the **Keepers of the Open Ledger**. When a wayfarer comes home, they read their journal into the Ledger: relics found, places seen, glyphs learned, spells drawn, leads still open. Copies travel between Reliquaries by caravan. A new wayfarer can read everything their predecessors recorded.

What isn't written down is lost. Anything learned after your last visit to a Reliquary lies in your waycairn with your map, which is the best reason there is to make the long walk back for it.

## 6. Sanctuaries: why they're safe

**The truth:** every sanctuary is built around a **hearthstone**, a stone where people have gathered, cooked, sung, traded and told stories for so long that it's the most listened-to place for miles.

**Why monsters stay out.** Far from people, the Listening's attention drifts, and things there go **wild** (see *Wilding* below). A wild creature finds a well-heard place uncomfortable: too bright, too loud, like an owl in a lit kitchen. It keeps clear of a hearthstone's reach, which is roughly as far as the town walls.

Most sanctuaries add a second reason, which the world graph records:
- **Hearthward:** the hearthstone alone. Common in old, busy towns.
- **Patron:** a friendly spirit has adopted the town (a river spirit, an old stag, a cloud that sits on the bell tower) and shoos wild things away.
- **Garrison:** the **Hearthwardens**, a sworn company of guards, keep the gates.

**Graves are never inside sanctuaries**, because nobody ever falls in a place this well loved.

### Wilding (why danger rises with distance)
Places that haven't been listened to in a long time grow **wild**. Beasts grow big and bold. Constructs keep doing their old jobs badly. Plants get ambitious. The farther a place is from the Old Hearth, the longer it has gone unheard, and the wilder it is. That is the in-world reason difficulty rises as you push outward, and why re-hearing the world (exploring it, naming it, writing it down) is the great work of the present age.

## 7. The origin sanctuary: the Lanternfold

Every journey begins at **the Lanternfold**, a market town tucked into a fold of green Wendle meadowland at the center of the known Wide (world position 0, 0).

- **The Old Hearth** stands in the town square: the first hearthstone relit in the Rekindling (see *History*). It is never cold. Children dare each other to touch it, and it's always just warm enough to be disappointing.
- **The Long Rail** runs beside the gate, hung with hundreds of lanterns. Every new wayfarer lights a lantern there before setting out, **from the flame of the last wayfarer's lantern**. When a wayfarer comes home, they hang their lantern back on the rail. When one doesn't, the lantern burns down and its flame passes to the next.
- **The Mother Ledger**, the first and largest Reliquary, sits in a round library beside the Hearth.
- **The Unfinished Map** covers a whole wall of the map house. Every wayfarer who returns adds what they found. There is still far more blank than ink.
- The meadows around it are tame (tier 0), the bread is excellent, and the Wendle innkeepers will not let you leave without a pie.

## 8. History

Four eras. The world graph generates its ~20 skeleton events from these, and every region can echo any of them.

### The Lamplit Age (about 1,200 to 600 years ago)
The age of wonders and long roads. **Wren the First Scribe** discovered that the world listens and drew the first sentence it answered. Glyphcraft spread fast. The cultures of the Wide bound themselves together in the **Concord of Hearths**, a league of cities joined by the **Long Roads**, lit end to end by lamp-towers. The Teskari built bridges between mountain peaks. The Solanne sailed ships of glass. The Teskari bellfounder **Oren Kel** cast **the Morning Bell**, which could be heard in every valley of the Concord at once.

*Echoes:* lamp-tower ruins, Long Road milestones, Concord seals, glass shards on far beaches, half-finished wonders.

### The Quarrel (about 600 to 520 years ago)
A short, bitter war, and the darkest chapter in the Wide's memory. A school of glyph-scholars called **the Imperative**, led by **Corvane the Loud**, argued that the Listening should be *commanded*, not asked. Corvane wrote **the Iron Sentence**, a glyph meaning "you will," and built tireless constructs to enforce it. The Concord fought back. The war ended at **the Silent Field**, where so many sentences were shouted at once that the Listening simply turned away from everyone.

*Echoes:* old battlefields, rusted constructs still guarding their posts, broken bridges, Imperative vaults, fragments of the Iron Sentence.

### The Hush (about 520 to 90 years ago)
After the Silent Field, the world stopped listening closely. Glyphs answered weakly. Relics slept. The lamp-towers went dark one by one. People drew back into a handful of safe valleys, the Long Roads grew over, and the wild crept in.

But the Hush was also an age of small things done well: village festivals, family crafts, songs handed down, recipes guarded like treasure. Those small, loved things are what kept the world's ear from closing completely.

*Echoes:* abandoned villages, hidden caches, songs everyone sings slightly wrong, a sudden flowering of local crafts.

### The Rekindling (about 90 years ago to now)
A retired Wendle shepherd named **Maudry Tull** sat down at a cold hearthstone in a meadow fold and told stories aloud to anyone who'd listen, every night, for a year and a day. On the last night, the stone was warm. The Lanternfold grew up around it.

The world is turning back toward its people. Glyphs answer again. Relics are waking up in attics and ruins. Hearthstones are being relit one by one, and each new sanctuary is a celebration. **Wayfarers** now set out from the Lanternfold to find what was lost: the old roads, the old wonders, the cultures that drifted apart in the Hush, and whatever lies past the edge of the Unfinished Map.

*The present:* the Wide is huge, half-forgotten and waiting. There is so much more to explore than anyone thought.

**The Great Wager:** forty years ago, Captain Corro and Hjalla Osvik bet a barrel of good cider on who could reach the farther edge of the Wide. Neither has come home. Letters from both still turn up now and then, carried by caravans from impossible distances. Each one claims to be winning.
