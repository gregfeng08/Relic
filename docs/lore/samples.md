## Cultures

### The Nel (Nel)
*[northern herders and storytellers · hearth names · Frost, Highlands]*

Northern herders and storytellers who measure winters in tales. Their herds know the way home better than most maps. Some halls stand roofless on the old ridges, and travelers still knock before entering.

- **People:** Hodlywick, Hodneock Fenmot, Hodkin Fenby. **Places:** Betricley, Nelbyfold, Edriccombe.
- **Crafts:** wool-felting, fur-lined leatherwork, sledge-building, ice-fishing. **Materials:** amber, elkhide, felted wool, reindeer antler, rime-iron, iron.
- **Makes:** bearded axe, antler-hilted knife, sledge-spear, fur cloak, horn, saga-staff, round shield. **Marks:** a twist of red wool tied through a hole made for it; a knot carved in where the hand rests.
- **Values:** a story told well, endurance, sharing the warm spot by the fire. **Festivals:** the Herd-Coming (spring), Sun-Return (midwinter).
- *Trade with Tel:* Every market day the Tel bring date cakes; Nel sledge-building goes back the other way.
- *Wary with Mabdryish:* Polite with the Mabdryish, but they count the change twice.

### The Rue (Rue)
*[woodwrights and grove-tenders · grove names · Forest, Meadow]*

Patient forest folk who grow their houses instead of building them. The Rue speak softly in the woods, because the woods are listening. Their children learn the names of trees before the names of towns.

- **People:** Oan, Suvioru, Mevean. **Places:** Seviavel, Suveira Rootway, Teilesse.
- **Crafts:** moth-silk weaving, mushroom farming, woodcarving, lacquerwork. **Materials:** yew, river pearl, birch bark, living willow, silver.
- **Makes:** carved longbow, moth-silk cloak, seed-pouch charm, bark-bound tome, antler knife, wooden flute, woven shield. **Marks:** a drop of red lacquer shaped like a seed; a leaf carved so finely it seems to move.
- **Values:** taking only what you need, patience, care for living things. **Festivals:** First Leaf (spring), Moth Lantern Night (late summer).
- *Trade with Bramwin:* Every market day the Bramwin bring pear cider; Rue woodcarving goes back the other way.
- *Kinship with Tesinean:* Many families have cousins among the Tesinean, and visit at harvest.
- *FriendlyRivalry with Corleyish:* Every few years a race against the Corleyish; the loser hosts the feast.
- *FriendlyRivalry with Tel:* A long, friendly argument with the Tel about who makes the better date cakes.
- *FriendlyRivalry with Mabdryish:* Every few years a race against the Mabdryish; the loser hosts the feast.

### The Bramwin (Bramwin)
*[orchard-keepers, beekeepers and cider-makers · hearth names · Meadow, Coast]*

Valley folk who run their lives by the blossom and the harvest. Their valleys hum with bees from spring to first frost. The Bramwin measure time in blossoms and harvests, never in hours.

- **People:** Dab Maudric, Mabrokin Rudwick, Oswick. **Places:** Maudett Hollow, Rudrowmere, Osbyford.
- **Crafts:** grafting, candle-making, jam-making, basketry. **Materials:** oak, willow, hawthorn wood, beeswax, brass, oak and pewter.
- **Makes:** pruning knife, pruning staff, cider chalice, music box, hourglass, harvest smock, recipe book. **Marks:** a tiny bee pressed in where the hand rests; the year's vintage scratched small and neat.
- **Values:** planting for later, generosity, patience. **Festivals:** the Pressing (autumn), Blossom Day (spring).
- *Trade with Rue:* Every market day the Rue bring smoked river fish; Bramwin candle-making goes back the other way.
- *Kinship with Tesinean:* Old friends of the Tesinean; their festivals share an evening.
- *Trade with Corleyish:* Bramwin basketry for the Corleyish hard cheeses, and both sides think they got the better deal.

### The Corley (Corleyish)

## Prehistory

### The Kindling Dawn (985–585 years ago, bright)

The first Tesinean families lit their hearths at Renezo Light, and Ottaina taught them to listen to the things they loved. *Echoes:* first hearthstones, founders' cairns, the oldest songs, worn stone thresholds, the first maps, mostly blank.

- **Edbet Jemton the First Teller** (founder): Edbet Jemton founded Betricley and was famous there for sharing the warm spot by the fire long before anyone called it a Nel virtue.
- **Mesa the First Tender** (founder): Mesa founded Seviavel and was famous there for taking only what you need long before anyone called it a Rue virtue.
- **Dabbet Edny the Generous** (founder): Dabbet Edny walked the first Bramwin families to Maudett Hollow, carrying a music box that never let them lose the way.
- **Ottaina the Lamp-Lighter** (founder): Ottaina founded Renta Light and was famous there for a well-told story long before anyone called it a Tesinean virtue.
- **Wilric the Hearth-Lighter** (founder): Wilric founded Lotalford and was famous there for honest work long before anyone called it a Corleyish virtue.
- **Irim Qaim the Generous** (founder): Irim Qaim founded Omesh Kiln and was famous there for reading the sky long before anyone called it a Tel virtue.
- **Fenmot the Merry** (founder): Fenmot walked the first Mabdryish families to Pellafold, carrying a reed shield that never let them lose the way.
- **Fenmot's Feast** (710 years ago, bright): Fenmot threw a feast at Ala Green to celebrate the first catch, and it has been held every year since.
- **The Honey Market at Ala Green** (635 years ago, bright): Traders of House Tiba, Brewers met at a crossroads to swap honey, and Ala Green grew up around their stalls.
- *Relic:* **The Patient Wooden Flute**. A wooden flute from Seviavel, made in the Kindling Dawn and passed hand to hand ever since.
- *Relic:* **Wilric's Hand-Bell**. A Corleyish hand-bell made at Lotalford in the Kindling Dawn, so well loved that the world began to answer it.
- *Relic:* **The Clay Flask of the Sunbeam**. The clay flask Irim Qaim carried in the Kindling Dawn. It has been used every day since, and it hums about it.

### The Grey Hush (585–390 years ago, somber)

Roads grew over and relics slept, but the Bramwin kept their recipes and their songs. *Echoes:* abandoned villages, hidden family caches, songs everyone sings slightly wrong, overgrown roads, guarded recipes.

- **The Road to Yad** (555 years ago, bright): A band of Tel families crossed into the lands around Nelbyfold and settled at Yad, bringing their recipes with them.
- **The Silent Expedition to Osbyford** (410 years ago, somber): House Wenkin of Osbyford expedition to Osbyford went quiet; its last letter mentioned the Harvest-Home Hourglass.
- *Relic:* **The Harvest-Home Hourglass**. A hourglass from Osbyford, made in the Grey Hush and passed hand to hand ever since.

### The Long Lantern Years (390–90 years ago, bright)

Lamps were lit along every road, and Wilric's heirs made things the world still remembers. *Echoes:* lamp-tower ruins, old road milestones, peak-to-peak bridges, half-finished wonders, bells that still carry.

- **Saun and Azesh Omim** (375 years ago, bright): Saun and Azesh Omim spent a lifetime trying to make the finer astrolabe, and their workshops still face each other across the square.

### The Rekindling (90–0 years ago, bright)

Irim Qaim's old hearth was relit at Omesh Kiln, and wayfarers set out from it to rediscover the world. *Echoes:* newly relit hearthstones, wayfarers' waycairns, fresh lines on old maps, reopened roads, festivals revived after centuries.

- **The Revival of the Renta Light Lanterns** (85 years ago, bright): After 60 quiet years, Cesine's Yard brought back the old lantern festival of Renta Light.
- **Renine Cora's Discovery** (55 years ago, bright): Renine Cora found the Spyglass of Renta Light in the ruins at Renta Light, and the archivists are still arguing about it.
- *Relic:* **The Spyglass of Renta Light**. A Tesinean spyglass made at Renta Light in the Rekindling, so well loved that the world began to answer it.

## A festival

**The Lantern Night at Bramford** (Corleyish, late autumn)

Every household hangs one lantern for each family member who traveled that year, and the whole valley glows. The custom here goes back to Pipwick Pipwin, who first served hard cheeses to the whole of Bramford and refused to stop. This year the Merry Company of Dabockthorpe are in charge, and the prize is a wool cloak marked with a wheat-ear stamped where the hand rests.
