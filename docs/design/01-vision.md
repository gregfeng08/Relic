# 01 — Vision & Pillars

Status: **DRAFT — needs Greg's review** · Last updated 2026-10-12

## The one-hour feeling

> "Wow, there's so much more to explore than I initially thought, and I want to explore it more and find out all of its secrets."

Every feature is judged by whether it makes this sentence more true.

## Pitch

Relic is an action RPG set in an endless, procedurally generated world. You explore Genshin-style landscapes, uncover ruins and dungeons, and fight hard bosses for loot. The best loot is **relics**: unique items with lore, custom enchantments and a history that points back into the world. Each relic is a lead to somewhere you haven't been yet.

## Inspirations (what we take from each)

| Game | What we take |
|---|---|
| Realm of the Mad God | Loot grubbing, dangerous bosses, the rush of a rare drop, difficulty that rises as you push outward |
| StarBreak | Skill over stats: a stock character can clear the hardest content if the player is good enough |
| Wynncraft | Cities and sanctuaries: safe hubs to live, trade and store loot, scattered through a dangerous world |
| Genshin Impact | Terrain, traversal and the urge to see what's past the next ridge; art direction |
| League of Legends / Genshin | Deep, connected lore with factions, history and characters |
| Minecraft | An endless world from a seed; layered-noise terrain |
| D&D | Imagination: anything can be in the world, and the world reacts to you |

## Pillars

Each pillar comes with a test question for judging a feature.

### 1. Secrets all the way down
The world has depth: the closer you look, the more you find. A ruin has an inscription, which points to a hidden vault, which holds a relic whose lore names a place three regions away. Discoveries come in layers, not as one-off markers.
**Test:** *Does this give the player a reason to look closer or go further?*

### 2. Loot that leads somewhere
Relics are breadcrumbs, not just stat sticks. Their lore refers to real places, factions and people in the generated world, and following those leads uncovers more. Common loot keeps the RotMG grubbing loop going. Relics are the moments you remember.
**Test:** *Does this item make the player curious about the world, not just stronger?*

### 3. Bosses worth bragging about
Fights are skill tests with readable patterns. Bosses guard the best secrets and the best drops. Difficulty rises as you push farther from the origin, the way RotMG gets harder toward the realm's center.
**Test:** *Would a player tell a friend about beating this?*

### 4. Generated, never random
Everything comes from one shared set of world facts (the **world graph**). Terrain, dungeons, items, NPCs and lore all refer to the same facts, so secrets connect and players can follow them. A procedural world that contradicts itself has nothing to discover.
**Test:** *Does this agree with, and add to, what the world already says?*

### 5. The chase never ends
Build crafting, item memory, and an endless map give long-term goals without a level cap wall. There is always a better build, a deeper region and an undiscovered relic.
**Test:** *Does this give a player something to want next week?*

### 6. Skill is the only gate
Nothing is level-locked. A skilled player can sprint from the starting sanctuary to a deadly far-off city with starter gear, the way you can run from Mondstadt to Liyue at level 5. It's harder, not forbidden. Gear widens your options and adds a moderate amount of power. It never turns a fight from impossible into trivial. Farther regions are more dangerous because of enemy *behavior* (patterns, density, combinations), not inflated health and damage numbers.
**Test:** *Could a great player with starter gear get through this?*

## Non-goals (for now)

- Turn-based combat (D&D *systems* yes, D&D *turns* no)
- Level gates, character levels that lock content, or numbers that grow without limit
- Classes. One hero, and the build comes from gear.
- Crafting or survival busywork (food and water meters)
- Handcrafted main story. Story emerges from the world graph.
- Multiplayer in the first milestones. Generation stays deterministic so it can be added later.

## Name hook

The game is called **Relic**, so relics are the heart of it. One idea: a **Reliquary**, the player's collection hub and codex. Every relic you've found is listed there with its lore, the places it mentions, and which of those you have and haven't visited. Your Reliquary is a map of secrets you haven't solved yet.
